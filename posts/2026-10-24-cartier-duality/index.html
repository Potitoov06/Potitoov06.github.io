Line bundles

Throughout, \(S\) is a scheme and all group schemes are commutative.

Definition 1 (Line bundle) Let \(X\) be a scheme. A line bundle on \(X\) is a locally free sheaf of rank 1 on \(X\), i.e. a quasi-coherent \(\mathcal{O}_X\)-module \(\cL\) such that for every \(x \in X\) there exists an open neighborhood \(U \subseteq X\) of \(x\) such that \(\cL|_U \cong \mathcal{O}_U\). The set of isomorphism classes of line bundles on \(X\) forms a group under tensor product, called the Picard group of \(X\), denoted \(\operatorname{Pic}(X)\).

Definition 2 (Cartier dual) Let \(G\) be a commutative \(S\)-group scheme. Its Cartier dual is the fppf sheaf of groups on \((\mathrm{Sch}/S)\) \[ G^D := \underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{S\text{-gr}}(G, \mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}), \qquad T \longmapsto \operatorname{\mathscr{H}\!\mathit{om}}_{T\text{-gr}}(G_T, \mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}{}_{,T}). \]

Theorem 1 (Cartier duality) Let \(G\) be a finite locally free commutative \(S\)-group scheme. Then \(G^D\) is representable by a finite locally free commutative \(S\)-group scheme, of the same rank as \(G\), and the evaluation pairing \(G \times_S G^D \to \mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}\) induces an isomorphism \(G \xrightarrow{\sim} (G^D)^D\).

Proof. We only sketch the argument. The question is local on \(S\), so take \(S = \operatorname{Spec}R\) and \(G = \operatorname{Spec}A\) with \(A\) a finite projective \(R\)-module carrying a commutative and cocommutative Hopf algebra structure. Group homomorphisms \(G_T \to \mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}{}_{,T}\) correspond to grouplike elements of \(A \otimes_R \mathcal{O}(T)\), which in turn correspond to \(R\)-algebra maps \(A^\vee \to \mathcal{O}(T)\), where \(A^\vee = \operatorname{\mathscr{H}\!\mathit{om}}_R(A,R)\) carries the dual Hopf algebra structure. Hence \(G^D = \operatorname{Spec}A^\vee\), and \(A \to A^{\vee\vee}\) is an isomorphism because \(A\) is finite projective.

Example 1 For \(n \geq 1\) we have \((\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})_S^D \cong \mu_{n,S}\): a homomorphism \(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}\to \mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}{}_{,T}\) is determined by the image \(u \in \mathcal{O}(T)^\times\) of \(1\), subject only to \(u^n = 1\). By Theorem 1, also \(\mu_{n,S}^D \cong (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})_S\).

Remark. Over \(S = \operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{F}_p\), the group \(\mu_p\) is infinitesimal while its dual \(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\) is étale; so, unlike rank, connectedness is not preserved by duality.
